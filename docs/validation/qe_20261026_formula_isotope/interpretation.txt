Technical limits

Existing generic models already use raw isotope-neighbour evidence; PE has a
rough carbon-only envelope check. This pilot adds headgroup/adduct-aware atom
counts and exact inversion of log1p intensity differences. It does NOT discover
new isotope peaks. Existing channels are upper13C, lower13C and lower2x13C,
charge-aware; there is NO upper-M+2 measurement in these descriptors.
Nominal full-element M+1/M+2 totals bound descriptive compatibility only:
fine isotope components and resolving power are unknown. Cl37 or S34 must not
be claimed observed merely because a 2x13C neighbour exists. Same-formula
isomers have identical predicted ratios and cannot be separated by this test.

Unsupported/labelled/spiked formulas remain unknown by design; the endogenous
core candidate pools happened to have full formula coverage. Original full
manual denominators include all standards and unresolved rows, excluding X4
only. Coverage of a manual peak is not a new annotation and may be absent if
that correct peak was never generated as a candidate.

Independent checker uses a separate raw TSV parser, native affine target-mass
arithmetic, parsed adduct expressions, isotope polynomial convolution and direct
expm1 ratio inversion. All33 passed; 93 focused tool tests and formatting/lint
checks passed. Sources were frozen before pilot execution. No query manual
labels, saved curator RT fits or original feature identifiers were numerical
predictors. All source quantification and accepted calls remained unchanged.
These repeatedly exposed development datasets are not untouched validation;
manual absence means unconfirmed, not proven false positive or analytical FDR.

Next: inspect assignment/competition bottlenecks without altering calls. In
particular, a label already assigned confidently on another peak can block an
otherwise unused peak under the current one-pass competition. First quantify
the available correct-original-peak opportunities and unconfirmed/wrong options;
do not assume joint assignment will help or silently promote weaker labels.
